As we slip into the gray of winter, I already find myself longing for spring. Though the next season’s turn feels like little more than a distant glimmer of hope, I find solace in the comforting grace of Eveline Hasler and Käthi Bhend’s In My Dreams I Can Fly. A troupe of tiny creatures—a grub, two worms, a beetle, and a caterpillar—prepares for winter in their network of underground tunnels. The grub keeps a huge onion locked away in his storeroom for them to feast on, but the beetle cannot resist sneaking in and taking a small nibble. This small act of transgression drives a steady sequence of change; by the time spring has arrived, more than one transformation has occurred. Hasler portrays her characters with sympathy and and affection, observing strength and respecting sadness but never condemning weakness.
