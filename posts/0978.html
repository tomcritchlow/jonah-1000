The gifts we collect along the way will aid us on our journey. Here in the very heart of Search for the Lost Husband Week, I am shifting from East of the Sun, West of the Moon adaptations to other variants. Tanya Robyn Batt and @nicolettececcoli create an appealing folkloric blend in The Princess and the White Bear King. A princess dreams of a golden crown, which she later discovers in the possession of a white bear. She becomes his wife but soon loses him to a terrible curse when she looks upon his true form by candlelight in their bedchamber. She must follow him in a difficult quest, traversing strange lands, climbing a glass mountain with iron claws on her hands and feet, and outwitting a shrewd troll queen. Batt’s engaging prose and Ceccoli’s stylized illustrations bring the tale ably to life. A note for folklore enthusiasts: In the back matter, Batt provides a detailed description of her influences, listing “East of the Sun, West of the Moon”, “The Black Bull of Norroway”, and “White Bear King Valemon” as sources.
