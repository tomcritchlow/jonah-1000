Puss in Boots Week is half done, but I’ve got plenty of treats in store! Today’s book comes to us from John Cech and Bernhard Oberdieck. The two complement each other’s styles well, with Oberdieck’s merry, bright-hued illustrations in watercolor, pen, and ink (particularly the cheekily winking cat) enhancing the jocund tone of Cech’s writing voice. The typical denouement, in which the cat eats the ogre-turned-mouse, is softened to the villain’s irreversible confinement in rodent form. A note for folklore enthusiasts: the back matter compiles a far-ranging history of the Puss in Boots tale type, as well as the image of cats in world traditions.
