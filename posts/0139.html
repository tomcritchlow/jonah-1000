It’s a quiet and comfortingly familiar brand of magic that’s explored in Nancy Willard and Ilse Plume’s Night Story. A child tells of his experience traversing the landscape of dream in a journey of free-association through “the country where nothing lasts,” riding by train past a range of mountains, wandering into a starlit forest, sailing on horseback over a sea of cloud, and returning to his very own room to greet the rays of the morning sun. In her first-person narrative, Willard employs a pensive and poetic tone, beginning the book with the word “And” to suggest a story mid-flow.
