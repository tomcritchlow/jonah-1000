The Magic Wood

by Henry Treece · illustrated by Barry Moser

ISBN 9780060208035 · Find on Bookshop.org

If you’ve ever wished for the experience of slipping off among the trees and having a brush with something dark, dangerous, and altogether otherworldly, you can’t go wrong with Barry Moser’s haunting version of The Magic Wood, a poem by Henry Treece. A child meets a mysterious man in the forest who immediately appears entertaining yet unsettling. He can catch a lark and draw a dragon in the air, yet both his eyes are glass and his teeth can be seen through the back of his head. Cleverly, the child provides a false name and ultimately says a prayer to ward off the man’s eldritch influence. Interspersed with the quatrains is the nerve-jangling refrain: “The wood is full of shining eyes,/The wood is full of creeping feet,/The wood is full of tiny cries;/You must not go to the wood at night!” Just who—or what is this man? We may never know.
