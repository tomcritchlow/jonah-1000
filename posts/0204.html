Boots and the Glass Mountain

by Claire Martin · illustrated by Gennady Spirin

ISBN 9780803711105 · Find on Bookshop.org

Here is a fairy tale replete with grandeur and delight: Claire Martin and Gennady Spirin’s Boots and the Glass Mountain. Heedless of his older brothers’ taunts, a young man tames three magical horses, dons three fine suits of armor, and rides three times up a perilously smooth slope of glass to win a princess’s hand in marriage. Martin renders the plot in engaging, straightforward prose, while Spirin’s characteristically resplendent paintings lend the proceedings an air of old-world grandeur. A note for folklore enthusiasts: this book was adapted from “The Maiden on the Glass Mountain”, a tale in Peter Christen Asbjørnsen and Jørgen Moe’s Norske Folkeeventyr. The plot remains quite faithful to the original story, with the notable addition of a pack of trolls to serve as antagonists. In the Norwegian text, the hero is named “Askeladd” or “Askeladden” (literally “Ash Lad”), a male counterpart to Cinderella who appears throughout Norse folklore.
